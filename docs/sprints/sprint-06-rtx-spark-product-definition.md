# Sprint 06 — RTX Spark Appliance™ Product Definition

## Sprint objective

Create the canonical, bilingual product definition for RTX Spark Appliance™ so every subsequent product page, architecture diagram, industry solution, product brief and investor narrative is built from one approved source of truth.

This is a product-definition sprint. The primary deliverable is not a redesigned page.

## Business outcome

At the end of Sprint 06, Spark AI should be able to answer consistently:

- What category does RTX Spark Appliance™ create or compete in?
- Which enterprises should buy it first?
- Which urgent business problem triggers a purchase?
- What is integrated inside the appliance?
- Where does the product boundary begin and end?
- Why is the integrated platform better than separate compute, storage, RAG and archival systems?
- Which claims are proven, planned or prohibited?
- What should an executive, architect, data leader and channel partner each understand?

## Product-definition hypotheses to resolve

### Category

Working category:

**Enterprise AI Cold Data Appliance**

The sprint must determine whether this category is sufficiently understandable and defensible for enterprise customers, ODM partners, government agencies and investors.

### Working product definition

**RTX Spark Appliance™ is a governed enterprise AI infrastructure platform that combines accelerated RTX compute, intelligent tiered storage, Enterprise RAG, AI Agent runtime and long-term archival to turn underused cold data into secure, reusable knowledge.**

This statement is a hypothesis until approved against product reality and evidence.

### Primary value proposition

Turn fragmented, long-retained enterprise data into governed AI knowledge without requiring customers to assemble and operate separate compute, storage, retrieval, agent and archival systems.

### Priority customer hypothesis

- Regulated enterprises with large volumes of long-lived unstructured data
- Government and public-sector organizations requiring sovereign deployment and durable retention
- Healthcare organizations managing imaging, records and clinical knowledge
- Financial institutions requiring attributable retrieval, auditability and controlled AI workflows
- Manufacturers with engineering, quality and operational knowledge trapped in files and archives

## Sprint workstreams

### 1. Customer and problem definition

- Define primary and secondary ideal customer profiles
- Identify economic buyer, technical buyer, security approver, operator and user
- Rank enterprise problems by urgency, value and product fit
- Define purchase trigger events
- Separate customer evidence from internal assumptions

### 2. Product boundary and architecture

- Define included hardware, software and services
- Define required external dependencies
- Map data ingestion, processing, retrieval, governance and archival
- Define on-premises, private environment and hybrid assumptions
- Identify unsupported or future capabilities

### 3. Capability and differentiation model

- Convert feature lists into customer outcomes
- Define the relationship between RTX compute, intelligent tiering, Enterprise RAG, AI Agents, governance and archival
- Compare against build-your-own infrastructure, storage-only appliances, GPU servers and cloud-only AI platforms
- Identify defensible integration advantages

### 4. Claims and proof governance

- Create a claims register with status: proven, evidence required, roadmap or prohibited
- Define evidence source and owner for each quantitative claim
- Review claims including `1 PB`, `50+ years`, `10–100×`, cost reduction, compliance and data sovereignty
- Prevent unverified performance or regulatory claims from entering public copy

### 5. Messaging system

- Define brand, category, headline, subheadline and elevator pitch
- Create role-specific value propositions
- Produce Chinese and English messaging with semantic parity
- Define terminology rules for AI Agent, Enterprise RAG, cold data, archival and governance

## Required inputs

- Current hardware bill of materials and configuration assumptions
- Storage tier and capacity specifications
- GPU and AI runtime specifications
- Archival technology and media lifecycle evidence
- Current RAG, agent and governance software scope
- Deployment and security architecture
- Existing customer, partner or pilot evidence
- ODM strategy and support responsibility
- Commercial model assumptions

Missing inputs must be logged explicitly. They must not be replaced with invented claims.

## Acceptance criteria

Sprint 06 is complete only when:

1. One canonical product definition is approved.
2. Product scope and external dependencies are explicit.
3. Primary customer and buying committee are identified.
4. The top five customer problems are prioritized.
5. Every public quantitative claim has an evidence status.
6. Product capabilities map to business outcomes.
7. Competitive alternatives and differentiation are documented.
8. English and Chinese messaging express the same product meaning.
9. Unknowns and roadmap items are visibly separated from current capability.
10. Sprint 07 can build the product-page information architecture without redefining the product.

## Out of scope

- Full product-page redesign or production build
- Broad Design Token refactoring
- Site-wide component migration
- Lighthouse optimization unrelated to product-definition work
- Bulk asset migration
- New industry pages
- Investor page redesign

Small prototypes or diagrams are allowed only when they help resolve product definition.

## Engineering support allowed in Sprint 06

- A structured content schema for the approved product definition
- A private or non-indexed review surface if needed for stakeholder approval
- Diagram prototypes required to validate product boundaries
- No broad visual-system cleanup
