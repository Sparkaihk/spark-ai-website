# SPK-000 — Spark AI Doctrine

**Status:** Foundational  
**Owner:** Spark AI Technology Limited  
**Scope:** Company strategy, product definition, architecture, design, communication and execution

## Purpose

This doctrine defines the durable principles behind Spark AI. Product roadmaps, architecture decisions, website narratives and commercial materials should remain consistent with these principles unless the doctrine is deliberately revised.

## Core conviction

Enterprise data does not lose strategic value when access frequency declines.

Long-retained documents, images, records, engineering files, operational history and institutional evidence may be cold from an infrastructure perspective while remaining valuable for knowledge, accountability, continuity and future AI use.

Spark AI exists to make that value governed, usable and durable.

## The eight doctrines

### 1. Cold data is dormant knowledge

Do not treat infrequently accessed data as a storage problem alone. Its future value depends on whether enterprises can discover, interpret, govern and reuse it.

### 2. AI without governance is incomplete infrastructure

Retrieval, models and agents must operate inside identity, access, policy, lineage, attribution, retention and audit boundaries.

### 3. Knowledge must remain attributable

Enterprise AI should show where information came from, which policy applied and how an answer or action was produced.

### 4. Compute and data placement are one system decision

AI performance, cost and control depend on where data resides, how it moves and when it is promoted across storage tiers.

### 5. Long-term memory is infrastructure

Archival is not an afterthought. Durable retention preserves institutional evidence, historical context and future model utility.

### 6. Integrated systems reduce operational uncertainty

Enterprises should not need to assemble unrelated compute, storage, retrieval, agent, governance and archival components before gaining value from retained data.

### 7. Architecture must be explainable

Every platform claim should map to a visible system boundary, component responsibility, data flow and operational owner.

### 8. Evidence precedes amplification

Claims about performance, cost, capacity, compliance, security and lifespan must be supported before they are promoted.

## Strategic implication

Spark AI is not positioned as a hardware catalogue, a generic AI application vendor or a storage-only company. It builds **knowledge infrastructure** for enterprises that need AI value without surrendering governance, data control or long-term institutional memory.

## Decision test

Before approving a product or communication decision, ask:

1. Does it make enterprise data more governable?
2. Does it make knowledge more usable and attributable?
3. Does it reduce integration or operational uncertainty?
4. Does it preserve long-term value?
5. Can the architecture and claim be explained with evidence?

If the answer is no, the decision is outside the doctrine or requires explicit justification.
