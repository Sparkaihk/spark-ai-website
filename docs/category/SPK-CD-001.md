# SPK-CD-001 — Enterprise AI Cold Data Appliance Category Definition

**Category owner:** Spark AI Technology Limited  
**Flagship product:** Spark Appliance™  
**Platform:** Spark AI Knowledge Infrastructure

## Category name

**Enterprise AI Cold Data Appliance**

## Category definition

An Enterprise AI Cold Data Appliance is an integrated infrastructure system that activates long-retained enterprise data for governed AI use by combining accelerated compute, intelligent data tiering, enterprise retrieval, agent runtime, governance controls and durable archival capabilities.

## The category problem

Enterprises retain large volumes of documents, images, records, engineering files, media and historical operational data. Much of this information is costly to search, difficult to govern consistently and disconnected from AI workflows.

Traditional approaches separate the problem across multiple categories:

- GPU servers provide compute but not knowledge governance or archival lifecycle.
- Storage systems retain data but do not reliably convert it into attributable AI knowledge.
- RAG software retrieves information but depends on external infrastructure and governance.
- Agent platforms automate tasks but require controlled enterprise knowledge and tools.
- Archival systems preserve records but may remain disconnected from active AI workflows.

The Enterprise AI Cold Data Appliance category unifies these responsibilities around one governed data-to-knowledge lifecycle.

## Category jobs to be done

1. Discover and ingest long-retained enterprise information.
2. Place data across appropriate performance, capacity and archival tiers.
3. Apply access, policy, lineage, retention and audit controls.
4. Retrieve attributable enterprise context for AI use.
5. Support controlled AI applications and agent workflows.
6. Preserve source evidence and long-term institutional memory.

## Required category capabilities

An offering should not use this category unless it meaningfully addresses:

- accelerated enterprise AI compute;
- intelligent tiered storage;
- enterprise-grade retrieval and knowledge services;
- controlled AI Agent runtime or integration;
- data governance and auditability;
- long-term archival lifecycle;
- integrated deployment and operational responsibility.

## Primary buyers

- CIO and CTO organizations modernizing enterprise data infrastructure
- Chief Data and AI Officers activating governed proprietary data
- Infrastructure and platform leaders reducing integration complexity
- Security and compliance stakeholders controlling AI data use
- Records, archive and knowledge leaders preserving institutional value
- Government and regulated-sector buyers requiring controlled deployment

## Priority use environments

- Healthcare imaging and clinical knowledge
- Government records and sovereign knowledge systems
- Financial research, evidence and regulated communications
- Manufacturing engineering and operational history
- Education and research collections
- Media archives and rights-managed content

## Category differentiation

The category competes against the combined cost and risk of assembling separate systems. Differentiation should be evaluated through:

- time to governed AI value;
- integration and operational responsibility;
- attribution and auditability;
- data-placement efficiency;
- deployment control;
- long-term retention and recoverability;
- ability to support repeatable knowledge workflows.

## Category boundaries

An Enterprise AI Cold Data Appliance is not automatically:

- a general-purpose GPU workstation;
- a storage appliance with an AI label;
- a standalone RAG application;
- an unrestricted autonomous-agent platform;
- a replacement for all enterprise data platforms;
- proof of regulatory compliance by itself.

## Canonical product relationship

> Spark Appliance™ is the flagship Enterprise AI Cold Data Appliance for Spark AI Knowledge Infrastructure.

## Claims requiring evidence

- Supported capacity and configuration
- Retrieval or inference performance
- Data-tiering cost impact
- Archival lifespan and environmental assumptions
- Security and sovereignty boundaries
- Regulatory and compliance mappings
- Availability, recoverability and operational service levels
